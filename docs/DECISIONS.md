## 2026-09-06 — CANON C4 ruled: convergence, not inheritance

**Decision.** The distinction thesis and Configuration Economics share the least-action-under-finite-budget root by CONVERGENCE, not by inheritance. Neither branch is downstream of the other. Ruled by Tom Cassidy: "convergence, not inheritance — correct."

**Why.** The texts show it. CE's keystone quotes DP §4.2, but CE's maintenance cost, its observer class, its floor and its finding that "a community is a learner" were each reached from the economics side and then recognised as the same shape. Inheritance would have to explain why only one of six shared commitments carries a citation. Convergence explains all six, and it is the stronger relationship: the same shape found twice from two domains is evidence the shape is real.

**What it changes.** CE keeps its April 2026 firewall — "CE survives if DP drifts" — unchanged, because an argument that stands on its own is worth more than one that stands on a sibling. The distinction thesis keeps CE as its ethics leg, because that is a claim about what this branch is for, not a claim CE has to co-sign. The re-coupling draft (`configuration-economics/src/content/open/NOTES-observer-architecture.md`) is right about the deepening and should say "for the deepening" in so many words; that edit belongs to the CE room.

**Where it is recorded.** `docs/CANON.md` §3 C4 (marked RULED 2026-09-06) and §4. The root itself is stated in `docs/canonical/shared-root.md` (branch `cs/927-distinction-thesis`, written by worker #927), whose §7 records this ruling as landed.

**Still open, and deliberately not ruled here.** Shared-root §7.2, whether CE's keystone should follow the Least-Time naming ruling and say time where it means what is spent; and §7.3, whether CE's `established` floor needs a line saying it is the one agreement no observer can revise.
