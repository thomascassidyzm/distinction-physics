# The floor and the observer — is CE's reachable set at odds with the ontology ruling?

*Written as a position and ruled the same day. Written 2026-09-06 to settle shared-root §4.2 and §7.3, the harder of the two questions C4 left open. Everything quoted is Tom's own text or the two repos' own text. The frame and the verdict are mine, marked as such, and built so Tom can accept or reject them in one sentence.*

*Status: **RULED 2026-09-06.** Tom accepted the frame in §2 and the consequence text in §6 in one word — "yes" — and that word is the whole of his ruling text; the argument below is this document's, adopted on his acceptance rather than authored by him. Landed as consequence 6 under CANON C1, as the correction to shared-root §4.2, and as no change to CE. The open edge named in §8, whether the upper storey of the floor is hard enough for CE when it binds only for the living class, was not put to him and is not ruled.*

## 1. The question as it was put

Shared-root §4.2, in full:

> CE's formal setup defines R(C, B, T) as the configurations "reachable from C using exergy ≤ B in time ≤ T, under physical and biophysical constraints." That is a third-person set. Value is then made observer-relative by slicing it to R_living. The floor, `binding-constraint`, is `derived`, and what it is derived from, `exergy-not-energy`, is `established` on classical thermodynamics with no observer in it at all.
>
> DP ruled on 2026-07-14 that "there is no coherence to the question 'reality' as actually is" and that ontology itself is an OLU construct. Under that ruling there is no observer-independent reachable set for CE to slice. There is only what bounded persistent observers can distinguish as reachable.
>
> Pushed: DP says the floor is where observation ends; CE says the floor is where physics ends, whether or not anyone observes it. CE needs the second reading, because a floor that is negotiable by observers is not a floor, and the whole point of `binding-constraint` is that it cannot be priced away. DP's ruling makes that floor an agreement among OLUs rather than a fact about the world.

And §7.3 asked: does CE's `established` floor survive the ruling as written, or does it need a line saying it is the one agreement no observer can revise? The recommendation was the line, in CE, and a relabel of the exergy grounding to "established for OLUs".

Both branches call this floor the thing that cannot move. If the ontology ruling makes it movable, one branch has to give.

## 2. The frame that settles it

The word doing the damage is "observer-independent". It is carrying two meanings, and the two branches are each using one of them.

**Meaning A: independent of any observer.** True from nowhere. The way things are whether or not anything could ever tell. This is the noumenal reading, the one C1 dissolved: "there is no coherence to the question 'reality' as actually is."

**Meaning B: invariant across the observer class.** The same for every observer like us, and revisable by none of them, because revising it would take a measurement no such observer can make. This is what "the laws of physics" means in the ruling itself: "the slice of the universe we experience as OLU is consistent with the laws of physics, precisely because we make observations the way we do as OLU."

Put those side by side and the question in §1 comes apart.

CE's floor needs meaning B. It does not need meaning A, and it has never used it. "A floor that is negotiable by observers is not a floor" is exactly right, and it is a claim about B: the floor must be one that no observer inside the argument can revise. Whether the floor is also true from nowhere is a question CE's argument never asks, because CE's argument is addressed to the living, and the living are all inside the class.

DP's ruling removes meaning A. It says nothing against meaning B. It relies on B: the ruling names the laws of physics as the thing the OLU slice is consistent with, and Axiom 1 is imported from thermodynamics. If the ruling dissolved class-invariants, it would dissolve Landauer's limit, and the distinction thesis would lose its own first axiom.

So the question "is the reachable set observer-independent?" has the same defect as the question C1 dissolved. It presupposes that the only alternative to "true from nowhere" is "negotiable". There is a third thing, and both branches are already standing on it.

## 3. Why the floor is unrevisable, in DP's own terms

This is the part that turns the frame from a distinction into a derivation. DP does not merely permit the floor to be class-invariant. DP's axioms say why it must be.

1. Every distinction costs energy. Axiom 1.
2. Every observer's budget is finite. Axiom 2.
3. So no observer can hold a distinction its budget cannot pay for.
4. The floor is the state where no distinction is maintainable at all: "heat death is the state without OLUs, where no distinctions are maintainable." Module 7.
5. To revise the floor, an observer would have to distinguish something past it: a configuration reachable beyond the point where distinctions can be held.
6. That distinction cannot be paid for by definition of the floor.
7. So no OLU can revise the floor. Not because it is true from nowhere, but because revising it requires ceasing to be an observer.

Maxwell's demon is the specimen. A less bounded observer appears to get a different second law until you charge it for the memory it holds, and the charge is Landauer's limit, which is Axiom 1. The second law is invariant across bounded observers precisely because distinctions cost energy. The floor's unrevisability is a corollary of the distinction thesis, not a casualty of its ontology ruling.

Both branches already say this and neither noticed it was the answer. Shared-root §2: "DP names it as the end of observation; CE names it as the end of reachability. Both refuse to let it be priced." The end of observation is why it cannot be priced. Those are not two readings that have to be reconciled. One is the ground of the other.

## 4. What "a fact about the world" means after C1

The last sentence of §4.2 says the ruling "makes that floor an agreement among OLUs rather than a fact about the world." That sentence is wrong, and it is wrong in the way C1 warned about. It treats "agreement" and "fact" as a downgrade and its opposite. Under the ruling there is no such opposite.

Least-Time Learning §3, Tom's frame: "A fact is an agreement — a first-party experience collapsed into a shared label." Tom on the name, 2026-08-04: reality as it actually is "is a human construct based on triangulation of first person experiences into third person 'realities' - i.e. the scientific method and so on."

So a third-person set is a triangulation. CE's R(C, B, T) is written in the third person of physics because that is what physics is: the class-invariant, triangulated form. It was never a view from nowhere. Calling it "an agreement among OLUs" does not weaken it. Every fact anyone has ever had is that.

What distinguishes the floor from every other agreement is not that it is more than an agreement. It is that it is the one agreement whose revision is unaffordable to every party to it. The scaling node has the same shape at a smaller scale: the number line is an agreement, and two is still twice one for everyone inside it. Nobody prices that away by declining to agree.

Tom's own words on the name settle the register. He asked for "ontological humility" and, in the same breath, "non-relativistic ethics". That pair only coheres if something is invariant without being noumenal. The floor is that thing. This document is the sentence that says how.

## 5. What is still real, and what it costs

I am not papering this over. Two things survive the frame and should be said.

**The floor has two storeys, and they are hard for different classes.**

- The lower storey is the second law: `exergy-not-energy`, Landauer, heat death. Invariant across every bounded observer. No OLU of any kind can price it away. This is where Axiom 1 lives and where CE's `established` label is earned.
- The upper storey is `binding-constraint`: sinks, materials at their regeneration rates, biospheric integrity. These bind for the living class specifically. A sink for waste heat is a sink for life at 300 K. Exergy itself is measured against a reference environment, and observers who share that environment agree on it.

The pricing-away threat CE fears, an observer who says "sinks are not binding for me", fails on both storeys but for different reasons. On the lower storey it fails by physics. On the upper storey it fails because the only observer who could say it is outside the living class, and CE's value is defined for the living class. CE's own `viable-objective` already makes this move: a no-regret rule for the living, not a theorem about the universe. The cost to CE is to own it in one line: the floor is as hard as the class is wide, and the class is the class the argument is for.

**Meaning B rests on the ruling's own text, not on ontic structural realism.** `philosophical-position.md` line 72 says "the relational structure that constitutes reality does not depend on any specific OLU observing it. If all humans ceased to exist, the structure would remain." That is a stronger claim than this document needs, and the July derivation chain called its ontological extension "an unearned extra". I have leaned only on the ruling itself: the OLU slice is consistent with the laws of physics because of how OLUs observe. Class-invariance is in that sentence. Nothing here requires the structure to persist with no OLUs at all, which is meaning A by the back door.

**What each branch gives.** DP gives up nothing, but owes the sentence. CE gives up the right to read its own `established` as true from nowhere, which it never cashed. Nobody's text has to move except the two sentences named in §6.

## 6. What to do, concretely

**CE: no change.** The recommendation in shared-root §7.3 to write "the one agreement no observer can revise" into CE, and to relabel the exergy grounding "established for OLUs", is the wrong direction, for two reasons.

- It breaks C4. The firewall says CE survives if DP drifts. Writing DP's vocabulary of agreement and revision into CE's floor imports DP's frame into the one node CE built to stand without it.
- The relabel would mark the floor as weaker than the rest of CE. Every `established` claim in any science is established for observers like us. Qualifying one node and not the others implies the others are more than that, which is the hubris the ruling names.

CE's floor is already in the class-invariant form. Physics' third person is that form. Leave it.

**DP: one consequence added to CANON C1.** The ruling that removed meaning A should say in its own list that it did not remove meaning B, and why. Proposed text, as consequence 6 under C1:

> 6. The ruling dissolves the question of reality apart from observation. It does not dissolve invariants across the observer class: those are what "the laws of physics" names in the ruling itself, and Axiom 1 is one of them. A fact is an agreement, and the floor is the one agreement no OLU can revise, because revising it would require holding a distinction past the point where distinctions can be held. CE's floor and the ethics leg's "denominator that does not move" are that agreement seen from the two sides of it.

That sentence belongs in DP because DP owns the ruling that raised the question. CE's lineage page can point at it for the deepening, which is the only coupling C4 allows.

**Shared-root: correct §4.2.** Replace the last sentence of the third paragraph, "DP's ruling makes that floor an agreement among OLUs rather than a fact about the world," with: "DP's ruling makes that floor an agreement among OLUs, which under the same ruling is what a fact about the world is. The floor is the one such agreement no OLU can revise." And mark §7.3 as answered: no line in CE, one consequence in C1.

**The one-line relation, unchanged.** Shared-root §5 stands. The distinction thesis is the theory of what one bounded persistent observer can hold and at what cost. CE is the theory of what a class of such observers should select. The floor is where holding stops, for all of them at once.

## 7. Verdict

Not a contradiction. Two descriptions at different levels, and the levels are these: CE states the invariant, in the third person physics uses for invariants. DP states what an invariant is for an observer like us, and its axioms say why this one cannot be revised. The question "observer-independent or not?" was malformed in the same way C1's question was malformed, and it dissolves the same way.

Neither branch has to give. If Tom disagrees, the place to disagree is §2: either meaning B is not really in the ruling, or CE's floor really does need meaning A. I have argued both are false, from the texts.

## 8. What this asks of Tom

One ruling, answerable in a sentence:

- **Accept the frame in §2 and the consequence text in §6?** Then C1 gains its sixth consequence, shared-root §4.2 is corrected and §7.3 closes, and CE is not touched. My recommendation is yes.
- Or: "not sure, keep exploring", which is a first-class answer. The open edge, if there is one, is whether the upper storey of the floor in §5 is hard enough for CE's purposes when it binds only for the living class. My read is that it is, because CE's value is defined for that class and no other.
