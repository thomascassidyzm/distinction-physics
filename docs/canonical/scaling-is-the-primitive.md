## 1. The ruling

> Nothing that humans observe ever relates to a position on a number line - thats just an agreement of how much more one is than something else.
>
> — Tom Cassidy, 2026-08-29

That is the headline claim and everything below hangs off it. What an observer actually meets is a comparison: this against that, how much more, how many times bigger. The number line is not what was observed. It is a shared arrangement of the names we give comparisons, and the numeral is the name.

[ASSEMBLER] The ruling is stated in Tom's terms on purpose. It is not to be clarified into place-value, number sense, proportional reasoning or ratio-and-proportion-as-a-topic, and "agreement" is not to be softened into convention or representation. Translating the frame is the failure mode the naming ruling warns about, and a frame error at the top ships as a product error at the bottom.

## 2. Comparison is a concept; the numeral and the number line are facts

This project already draws the distinction the ruling needs. From `docs/canonical/least-time-learning.md` §3: a fact is an agreement, a first-party experience collapsed into a shared label; a concept is a compression, a derivable relation that regenerates families of distinctions.

Apply it at the oldest agreement in the subject:

- **Comparison is a concept.** Derivable by anyone, anywhere, without being told. Language-independent, culture-independent, universal. Nobody has to be taught that one thing is twice the other; they have to be taught what to call it.
- **The numeral is a fact.** It is the agreed NAME for a comparison. Which mark, which word, which base — all agreement, all local, all learnable only by being told.
- **The number line is a fact.** It is an agreement about how those names are laid out relative to one another.

[ASSEMBLER] This is the naming ruling applied to maths' oldest agreement: grounded experience first, then understanding, then drill, then equivalence naming, with names and notation priced as purchases under cache policy. The rule is stated at `docs/canonical/least-time-learning.md` §6. The consequence for maths is that the numeral is a purchase, and it should be bought after the comparison it names has been felt, not before.

## 3. Discreteness licenses the placeholder

Tom's chain: discrete implies count; continuous implies scale. The placeholder — the numeral standing in for a comparison — is licensed by discreteness. Where the world comes in countable units, a name can stand in for the comparison without loss, because there is something to count. Where it is continuous, what is there is scale, and the comparison is the thing itself.

This is the point where the ruling connects to the framework's own structural result. Effective discreteness is derived from the two axioms — all distinctions cost energy, all observers have finite energy budgets — in `docs/derivations/effective-discreteness.md`, with the kernel statement at `src/content/treatise/module-0-philosophical/section-0.0.ts` and the axiom pair on `README.md`. The bounded observer resolves a continuum into discriminable steps because resolution is paid for. That is what makes counting available at all, and counting is what the placeholder rests on.

## 4. Addition is not a second primitive

Tom's derivation, carried as stated:

```
a + b = a(1 + b/a)
```

A sum with a base is a scaling by one-plus-a-ratio. Addition does not need its own primitive slot; given a base, it is already scaling.

- **The factored form is the estimation rung.** `a(1 + b/a)` is the form that answers the question that actually gets asked — is this a five per cent correction, or a doubling? The unfactored sum answers it only after the arithmetic is done. This lands directly on the ladder axis of sense → estimation → calculation, ruled by Tom and swept through the maths estate on 2026-08-24: "yes, sense is broader logic, followed by estimation, finally by calculation (broadly)" — `~/zenjin-2026-v1/docs/ladder-order-sweep-for-tom.md`.
- **The residue is symmetric aggregation.** Once a base is not available — two things thrown together with neither one the reference — what is left is aggregation, and counting is the floor of it. That residue is real, and it is small.

## 5. The floor finding, 2026-08-29

Tom corrected the reading of the 314-node maths graph the same day. The 314 nodes are **the floor**:

> The originals were largely to get people from zero maths knowledge other than counting… So all the doubling and halving and so on and then all the x tables.
>
> — Tom Cassidy, 2026-08-29

Why this belongs in this node: the floor is where scaling-as-primitive is **already implemented**. Doubling and halving are scalings. Times tables are the discrete case of the same relation. The ladder from zero maths knowledge to arithmetic is built out of the primitive this ruling names, and it was built that way before the ruling was stated.

The syllabuses start halfway up and assume the floor. That is why the GCSE claim extraction — 496 composite claims across seven boards, 425 of them new against the graph — measured a different layer and reported the difference as absence. The three orphan clusters no board claims, `HALVEODD`, `DECIMALSHIFTPOWEROF10` and `SCALEDETAILCOMPARE`, are representative of the 314 rather than anomalies inside it.

So the relations the specs teach separately have their common ancestor sitting in the existing floor nodes already. This is recorded as a dated finding. It is not a re-analysis of the graph, and no node, generator or question is touched by this document.

## 7. What follows for the rungs

**Wide rungs should be comparisons with NO NUMERALS IN THEM.** A wide rung is where the comparison is met, and the numeral is the name that gets bought later. Putting a numeral in a wide rung charges the agreement before the concept it names has been felt, which is the pricing rule broken in the ordinary direction.

**Measurement is itself a ratio.** Every measured quantity is a comparison against a unit, and the unit is an agreement. This is the same shape as §2, met in the world rather than on the page.
